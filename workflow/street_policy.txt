Street inclusion policy — classify the route before tracing its banks. First identify established streets from source evidence such as a mapped street continuing between blocks, documented street widths, and banks or street junctions independently visible elsewhere along its course. For an independent minor route (its own alignment and two sides outside an established street), exclude paths or service access whose own boundaries are predominantly dashed. An isolated system of dashed walks or planked access drives serving structures still counts as independent paths, even when one is long, named, of uniform width or joined by several similar branches. Dashes, access to buildings and connections among these paths do not on their own establish a street network. End a proposed or unfinished extension where the built street actually ends. Put excluded paths in nonroad, uncertain candidates in unresolved, and return an empty road inventory if appropriate.

Only after a street is established, trace its complete supported width through bends, junctions, side entrances and sheet exits. The independent-path test applies only beyond the parent street's outer bank: never apply it to the left or right part of an established street, to an intersection flare, or to a longitudinal stretch merely because bank ink changes style. Use mapped widths, block frontages, opposite banks and continuation on either side of a local break. Do not follow an interior rail, pipe or dashed marking as a substitute bank. Interpolate a short supported gap between bank endpoints and use fixed_edges where snapping would pull it inward. At the mouth of a rejected branch, omit the branch polygon and retain the full parent cross-section. Follow genuine width changes and evidenced physical obstructions.
